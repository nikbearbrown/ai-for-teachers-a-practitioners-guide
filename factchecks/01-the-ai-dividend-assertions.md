## Critical — Requires Immediate Expert Review

### CONTRADICTED — Bastani et al. PNAS "17 percentage points lower" framing

The chapter states (line 169): *"On the subsequent unaided exam, they scored 17 percentage points lower."* The Bastani et al. PNAS (2025) paper actually reports a **17% reduction** in scores for the GPT Base condition — a relative percentage change, not 17 percentage points (an absolute difference between two percentages). These are not the same. The chapter conflates "17%" with "17 percentage points." Cross-referenced in Chapter 13 for fuller treatment; flagging here because the unit error is load-bearing for the misconception #4 example. Recommend rewording to "17% lower" or "17% reduction" to match the paper's language.

### UNVERIFIED — "30%" weekly users vs. published 32%

The chapter states (line 40): *"About **30%** — call it three in ten — used AI **weekly**."* The Gallup news release headlines "Three in 10 Teachers Use AI Weekly" and reports the figure as **32%** weekly users specifically. "Three in ten" is a defensible rounding, but the 30% figure understates by 2 points. Suggest changing to 32% to match the primary source, or keep "three in ten" rhetorical phrasing without the precise 30%.

Bastani et al. report 17% reduction (relative), not 17 percentage points (absolute). The chapter's own methodology section (§5, misconception #4) is where precision matters most, since the misconception is about misreading numbers. A precision error in the very paragraph that warns about precision errors is the highest-priority fix. This is the only internal inconsistency identified.

The chapter rounds the Gallup figure down (30% vs. published 32%). Defensible as rhetorical rounding ("three in ten") but the precise number is 32%. If the chapter's pedagogical move is "be precise about numbers," using "32%" reinforces the lesson.

The PNAS published a correction (https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2518204122) on June 25, 2025. The correction is administrative (Osbert Bastani's institutional affiliation), not substantive. The chapter's footnote already cites the correction — good practice.
